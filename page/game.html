Underfoot / the game

Underfoot

Ashwake's grey dithered 3D and its near-overhead camera, pushed to a scale far past the Warden. You are one small person in a walled hill town. Something nearly three kilometres tall walks the valley with a lantern on a chain, and when it stops over the town, its light comes in low between the houses, always a few steps behind you. Keep to the shadows, which turn as the lantern moves.

Underfoot grey dither, one warm light

Jump to

WASD or the arrows to walk, or hold the mouse where you want to go. Hold L or the right button to look up. Hold the mouse on a lit house, or E at its door, to put its light out. Space, Enter or a click begins. M for sound. Drag on the picture to walk. Hold a finger on a lit house to put its light out. Hold the amber buttons to look up and to put a light out. Until you begin, the town plays itself.

The decision

The light is the whole game. It is held to one side and comes in at about 45°, so every house throws a long shadow, and the shadows swing as the lantern moves. It walks your trail about three seconds behind you, so it is always close. The scale is never shown all at once: you see its boots, its chain and its light, and the whole of it only when you look up, or when it finds you and the camera climbs from the roofs to its face.

How it's made

  • Ashwake's pipeline at 386×218: flat-shaded meshes lit in colour, then snapped to eight warm greys and four dull ramps (amber, moss, slate, rust). A pixel only takes a hue when it is coloured enough, so the town stays grey and the lantern, roofs and cloak carry the colour.
  • The play camera is a perspective 1.5 km away with a 2° field, near-orthographic like Ashwake's. Looking up and the catch are cuts through a short dip to dark, to cameras that open up to about 75°.
  • The giant is about 2.9 km tall: 23k triangles on 15 bones, skinned on the GPU. The feet follow a step plan, legs and arms solve with two-bone IK, and the head turns to where its light falls. Its face is a sculpted iron mask built from one height function; the shader reuses that function to darken the hollows and brighten the ridges.
  • The lantern trails the hand on a 1.9 km chain. It has its own shadow map, so the houses throw real shadows, and the hiding test traces three lines to it, so a shadow's edge is a band where you are half seen.
  • As it comes, cloud covers the sun and the lantern and a few windows are left. Sun shafts are marched in the palette pass; clouds are a raymarched slab; sound is WebAudio, all synthesized.

For your game

A hiding loop for something too big to fight: the light is the searcher, the houses make the shadows, and looking up is how you learn its size. Cellars, market halls and bridges are the next hiding places to add.

The current look’s palette

Everything is drawn and synthesized in code. The giant, the town and the lantern are original designs.
