# Underfoot: is it fun? (critique of 2026-10-22, build 8f3780c)

How this was made: four scripted critics (first minutes, the hook, the hunt, the design) each played the build headless through the test hook, driving eight kinds of player through more than 130 searches, and wrote up what is strong and what is weak with frames and logs. Each top weakness was then handed to two independent checkers told to refute it; 16 weaknesses survived. The lists below are the synthesis, in plain words. The measurements come from scripted players, not people; a human may do better than the bots, but the causes are in the rules and were checked in the code.

## Verdict

Underfoot looks and reads like a finished small horror game, and plays like a 20-second countdown. The best scripted players passed night 1 about one time in four and night 2 about one in ten; nobody passed night 3 or anything after it, including the game's own hider brain. Every death is the same chain: the light's centre rides about 7 m behind you; after 4 s in one spot your dark corner silently counts as half seen; half seen pulls the lamp nearly overhead, where no house throws shadow; and the hunt runs as fast as you. So the title's one instruction, "Stay in the dark", is the losing move, running in circles is the best strategy, the endless nights end at 2, and because a catch wipes the night, nearly every game-over card reads "Score 0. Best 0." It is fun for the ten seconds when the lamp is low and the pool slides between the houses, and for the one look up at the giant. It is not yet fun as a score game for a stranger on itch.

## What is strong

- The picture and the rule agree. Where the drawn pool's edge says you are half seen, you are (the three rays and the shader's soft edge match). A player who loses can see why, which is rare in small stealth games.
- The tells read at a glance at 480 x 270: the word over the helm (Hidden / Half seen / Seen), the eye bar in the tier's colour, the amber pool, the edge pulse, the one lantern label. In a run the state changed twenty times and every change was visible.
- The title says the rule in three lines and one picture. The stakes arrive fast: 13 s from the click to "It is looking", and the square is lit 6 s later. "The square is death" is learned on run one with no tutorial.
- The lamp really circles and drifts from low to overhead, so the shadow of your house turns under you. The fiction gives a reason to move; only the rules fail to reward it.
- The catch is short and honest: push in, "it sees you", the burn, cut to black, card at 5 s, no face. The retry is four seconds.
- Holding L for the giant from the street is the best picture in the game, and it stays the player's choice, which keeps the giant mysterious.
- The step over the town seen from the street (today's change) already beats the video it replaced: the sole's shadow sweeps the lanes as a hard edge while you stand in it.
- The rhythm of a search is right: a good player makes a necessary move every 4 to 6 seconds. The outcomes are wrong, the rhythm is not.
- Everything is deterministic and tunable from a dozen numbers at the top of game.js and one table in nights.js, and the test hook let four critics script 130 searches in an afternoon with no errors. Every proposal below can be measured the same day it is built.

## What is weak, worst first

1. Hiding is the losing move and nothing survives past night 2. From night 3 the light's centre walks at 5.0 to 5.4 m/s against your 4.6, with a short lag, so it sits about 7 m behind you. After 4 s in one spot every dark cell near it counts as half seen and the eye fills in about 12 s: a fixed fuse against a 40 to 52 s search. Every survivor in 130 searches was a runner. The survival table in the design doc (section 8) predates these rules and is no longer true.
2. Being seen is a death animation with input, not a chase. In 22 of 24 catches the light's centre was within 5 m. A slip of 1.5 s at 8 s of night 1 was a catch at 13 to 15 s every time. When it suspects you the lamp pulls in to 8 m, nearly overhead, where a 7 m house throws about one metre of shadow; its centre walks through walls at hunting speed while you go round them.
3. Almost every run ends on "Score 0". A catch wipes the night's seconds and they bank only when it leaves. The stranger's four night-1 deaths, at 17, 28, 29 and 27 s (two within 1.5 s of passing), all read "Score 0. Best 0." while the counter on screen had just ticked to 20. In practice there are three scores: 0, about 130, about 265.
4. The first real decision is a silent trap. "Half seen" appears while you stand still in deep shadow with nothing changed in the picture (the 4-second rule). The lamp pulls overhead 1.2 s after your first move, and the catch comes 5.6 s after the first warning word. Nothing on screen says the lamp pulls overhead when it suspects you, or that moving early along a wall is the counter.
5. Camping is a slow death after the clock you outlasted. A night-1 camper in a deep spot is peered at from 15 s and burned at 33 s, past the 30 s search, because the night cannot end while it has you and the peer fills the eye slowly. The HUD counts down a clock that cannot save you.
6. Half of a night is waiting. From the click: 13 s of approach with walking frozen under the wide boot shot, 5 s of descent where nothing can touch you, 30 s of play, 12 to 15 s of leave, 4 s of card. A night-1 death costs 13 s of approach for 15 to 22 s of play.
7. The score is a night counter in disguise. Score = the table's search seconds + 100 a night, seconds lost on a catch, so everyone who dies on night 3 scores 265. The only variance is overtime at half rate, which pays more to a player who is half seen at the end than to one never seen.
8. "Half seen" is one word for four different speeds of filling, from 18 s to 5 s, on the same 15-pixel bar, and the peer looks exactly like standing in the dark: a knight under the arcade roof in blue-black, "Half seen", the bar at half, nothing explaining why.
9. The leave is now clean but empty. The old cut-away showed the sole as a pale blur across the roofs (the legs-through-buildings the user saw). The street camera reads better, but the giant is never seen and nothing marks the step: no shake, no dust, no sound change.
10. The take, the only tutorial, almost never happens while you are alive. It fired in 4 of 35 searches, at 15 to 26 s; a stranger is dead by 13 to 23 s. The curious one is a 6-pixel figure at the screen's edge while the pool is elsewhere.
11. No verb beyond walking, and nothing pulls you anywhere. Looking up freezes walking, so it is a pure cost during the search. No object to reach, no door, no decoy, never a reason to be in the light. Every paying move was a reaction to a threat, never a plan.
12. Night 1 has no breath. The light starts 40 m off, but at 6.5 s its cone covers nine tenths of the open ground within 30 m of its centre, and the catch-up gear runs from the first frame. A still player at the spawn is lit at 5 s.
13. Three pictures contradict the words. The search opens with the town flooded amber under "Hidden" because the lamp is still high; each night opens "It is looking" on the wide shot where the knight is a 3-pixel dot; behind a house the cut hole makes the knight read as standing on the roof.
14. Nothing to post, compare or come back for, and the over card explains nothing. The best lives in one browser, there is no best night count, no share line, the same hunt for everyone; the card says "Nights survived: 0" to a first-timer and never names the cause, which the game knows at the catch. The credit still says "by ____".
15. The lamp is off screen two thirds of the time. The pool is always in frame, so reading the lamp's circle means reading shadow directions with no cue for where it is going.

## Proposals, in order

1. Make hiding true and being seen a chase you can win round a corner. These go together; a slower light alone was tried and failed. Cap the light's walk below your speed on every night (make later nights harder by search length, spin speed and fill, not speed). In deep shadow you leave no trail, so the light goes to where it last saw you and loops there. The 4-second rule counts only while you stand still and resets once you are out of its reach. When it suspects you the lamp pulls in to 24 m, not 8, so a house keeps about 3 m of shadow. When it has you it follows your trail two steps back, so it takes your corners instead of walking through walls. Release the alert a little sooner. Then re-run the scripted players. Targets: a careful player passes night 1 three times in four, the hider survives night 3 about half the time, night 6 rarely, and escapes after "Seen" rise from 1 in 10 to about 1 in 3 on night 3. Why it is fun: the three seconds after "Seen" become the best part of the game. About a day, with the re-measuring.
2. Keep what you earned, and say why you died. Bank the seconds as they tick (every 10 s, with a soft chime and a flash of the score plate); a catch keeps what is banked and loses only the rest. Drop the night bonus from 100 to 50 until nights are commonly passed. The over card: "Night 1." large, then "You lasted 17 s. Best 29 s.", then the score, then one cause line ("You stayed too long under it", "In the half light", "In the open", "It chased you"). Keep best nights beside best score. Fill in the credit. Why: dying at 25 s then beats dying at 8 s, which is the whole engine of a score game, and "New best" fires in the first session. Hours.
3. Warn before the peer bites. At 2 s under it: "It is close", the ring under the knight flickering, the chain quickening. At the bite, its own word, "It is peering", with the lantern dipping, so the cause is on screen. The rule at 6 s on night 1. One flat fill speed for half seen so the bar means the same thing every time. A camper who is only peered at when the clock runs out gets the leave at a price (seconds scaled by how much the eye filled) instead of a burn. Say "It is coming down" until the lamp is low, and keep the town dim rather than flooded, so "Hidden" is never shown over a picture that contradicts it. Hours.
4. Cut the waiting: death to decision in under ten seconds. A retry starts with the lamp already coming down (no approach, no wide boot shot; keep the full approach only for the first night after the title). The lamp down in about 3 s. Give the breath back: the light starts farther off on night 1, its far reach capped nearer, the catch-up gear off for the first 6 s. The leave 8 s on every night; the card 2.5 s. Check: a still player lit no earlier than 9 s on night 1. Hours.
5. Make the step felt (the follow-up to today's change). A rolling shake as the sole's shadow crosses your lane, fading over 2 s; a hard footfall at the landing with a low hit, a sheet of dust pushed along the street and dust falling from the roofs; the shadow band a step darker and sharper while it crosses; the lamp's glow swinging across the roofs; the horn or chain dropping as the shadow passes. Keep L as the voluntary look up, with the look camera high enough that the sole can never pass below it, and show "Hold L to look up" once on night 1's leave. Hours.
6. Show the take first. On night 1 the curious one steps out of the door nearest you at 1.5 s, the light's first target is him, and it takes him at 5 to 6 s, 20 to 25 m from you, in full view; only then does it pick up your trail. Draw him bigger with the candle glow doubled; after the take, zero the suspicion so the pause is a real breather. The rule is shown before it is applied to you. Hours.
7. A night to beat and a run with a name. An "Unseen." +50 on the night card when the eye never passed a tenth, "Grazed." +25 under a half; "Night 4. 2 unseen." on the over card. Seed the hunt from the date and show "Night of 22 Oct" on the title and the card, keeping best per day and all time. "C: copy" on the over card writes "Underfoot, 22 Oct: night 4, 2 unseen, 412." to the clipboard. Itch comments become comparisons of the same hunt, and there is a reason to come back tomorrow. Hours, after 1 and 2.
8. Lost candles: one reason to step into the light on purpose. Two or three candles per night in the open within 25 m of where the search starts. Walking over one is +20; while you hold it you are half seen wherever you stand, until you snuff it with Space, which ends the risk and banks the points. The first thing that pulls you out of a good spot by choice rather than by threat. A day, last, and only once hiding works.

Recommended order: 1 and 2 together, re-measure with the same scripts, then 3 and 4, then 5 to 8.
